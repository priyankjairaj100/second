Adversarial limits
- A true ridge floor does not prove all matrices inside the entry box are SPD.
- The verifier cannot prove caller-supplied interval, source, or ridge premises.
- Empty intersections and impossible pivots raise; they never vacuously accept.
- A wrong candidate remains conditional until every coordinate passes.
- Inclusion dominance fixes the candidate, grid, ridge, and operation schedule.
- Dominance assumes completed arithmetic; finite caps can still abort.
- Full-service completion and latency dominance do not follow.
- Existing spectral checks remain first; replacing them can lose coverage.
- Unknown/unproved provider bounds cannot be converted into usable intervals.
- A bank must use common retained-source identities across its providers.
- Current aggregate group digests can contain provider-specific proof hashes.
  They must not silently define cross-provider common target identity.
- Bank storage and deleted-record extraction can grow by the bank size.
- Exact singleton-prefix anchors require actual finite evaluation at that prefix.
  Ideal singleton intervals alone do not erase finite-execution discrepancy.
- Full grid-prefix banks can have exponential size.
- The same certificate bank is available to equally indexed fresh computation.
- No universal practical speedup or language-model coverage claim is established.
