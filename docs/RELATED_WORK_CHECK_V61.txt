Second: focused literature checks for the next manuscript revision
Checked 14 October 2026. These are primary-source abstract/bibliographic checks,
not a comprehensive novelty review or a completed comparison of implementations.
No empirical result, current protocol, model, or manuscript was changed.

1. Frantar, Ashkboos, Hoefler and Alistarh: GPTQ: Accurate Post-Training
Quantization for Generative Pre-trained Transformers (2022 preprint / ICLR2023).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17323
The authors describe one-shot weight quantization using approximate second-order
information. Their reported efficiency concerns quantization and inference.
Implication for Second: exact equality to Second's declared finite calibration
target must not be described as equality to stock GPTQ or evidence of comparable
large-model efficiency. Keep the mathematical relationship and empirical baseline
identity explicit; the existing matched sequential model is its own bound target.

2. Williams and Aletras: On the Impact of Calibration Data in Post-training
Quantization and Pruning, ACL2024, pages10100–10118.
https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.544/
DOI: 10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.544
They investigate calibration choices across methods, datasets, tasks and models,
and report substantial downstream-performance variation.
Implication for Second: a quality pass on one adaptive calibration root is a
useful development check, not a root-general quality guarantee. This supports
the planned independent-root and untouched-quality obligations; it does not set
our sample size, acceptance margin, or tell us whether the fixed teacher works.

3. Zhang et al.: Catastrophic Failure of LLM Unlearning via Quantization
(2024 preprint).
https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16454
The authors examine quantization after knowledge-unlearning updates and report
recovery of previously suppressed information.
Implication for Second: this concerns knowledge in trained parameters. Removing
a record's contribution from a specified calibration reconstruction is a different
counterfactual and supplies no pretraining-forgetting or privacy guarantee. Use
this as a scope distinction if discussing unlearning, not as an equivalent repair
baseline or an unsupported claim that Second solves quantization-induced recovery.
