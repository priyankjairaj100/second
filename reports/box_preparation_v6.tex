\begin{theorem}[Midpoint optimality for the interval envelope]
For a fixed interval $[l,u]$ and any scalar anchor $z$,
\[
 \max\{|l-z|,|u-z|\}\ge\frac{u-l}{2}.
\]
Equality holds at $z=(l+u)/2$.
The midpoint therefore minimizes each interval-based entry bound, including its fixed finite discrepancy.
Summing squared entry bounds gives no larger feature radius than another anchor under the same intervals.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The triangle inequality gives $u-l\le |u-z|+|l-z|$.
Each term is at most their maximum.
The midpoint attains both equal distances.
\end{proof}

The implemented hybrid uses this anchor whenever the parameter domain varies.
It removes the separate finite base evaluation for that branch.
The fixed-base branch retains its exact zero-error anchor.
The provider identity binds both branches.
Changing the policy requires index reconstruction.
This optimality concerns the rectangular interval envelope, not the smaller reachable feature set.

\note{Coverage is not acceptance.}
Any fixed anchor obeys the diameter lower bound
\[

A broad feature range therefore forces a broad uniform error.
More interval precision cannot remove that range.
If covered prefixes need different exact codes, one constant universal certificate cannot accept both.
Narrow box banks can help, but their preparation and state must be charged.
For $m$ independent binary coordinates, boxes narrower than their separation require at least $2^m$ boxes.

Interval propagation and the Gram inequality are established mathematical tools.
